1. Terms of service

1.1 What is licensed

A {{VENDOR_NAME}} licence grants the right to run the software and determines which features and limits are enabled. It is a cryptographic authorization and a feature gate. It is not a copy-protection mechanism for the platform itself, and it does not convey ownership of anything.

1.2 Enforcement is fail-soft, by design

Licence enforcement degrades rather than terminates. A lapsed or suspended licence blocks new packaging and new entitlement issuance; it does not stop a running deployment, and there are grace windows before any degradation takes effect. This is deliberate: a vendor able to halt your production is a single point of failure you should not be asked to accept.

1.3 Your data and your keys

The control plane runs in your infrastructure. You hold the signing root and the content keys. We cannot decrypt your artifacts, and we do not receive them. Loss of your key material is unrecoverable by us — that is a property of the design, not an operational shortcoming, and key custody is your responsibility.

1.4 Acceptable use

You may not use the platform to distribute malware, to conceal unlawful functionality from the people running it, or to evade sanctions or export restrictions. Protecting your intellectual property is the purpose; hiding harm from the operator of a machine is not.

1.5 Export control

The software is encryption software and is treated as controlled under the US Export Administration Regulations. You are responsible for compliance with export, re-export and transfer restrictions applicable to your deployments, including restricted destinations, end-users and end-uses. Tell us where you intend to deploy before contracting.

1.6 Warranties and the honest ceiling

We warrant that the platform performs the functions described in the documentation. We expressly do not warrant secrecy of software executing on hardware controlled by a third party. Client-side execution cannot provide secrecy against a party who can inspect the machine, and no configuration of the open or local tiers should be relied upon for it — those tiers raise the cost of reverse-engineering, they do not prevent it. Confidentiality against a host that owns the machine is addressed only by the split and enclave tiers, and the enclave tier's hardware attestation is not enforced in the current release.

2. Privacy

2.1 The short version

The licensing service receives metering signals only. Usage counts, licence identifiers, deployment identifiers and timestamps. It does not receive your keys, your entitlement secrets, your artifacts, or any content processed by them. A heartbeat containing anything secret-shaped is rejected before it is stored.

2.2 What is collected

  • Deployment records — an organization identifier, a deployment identifier, and the deployment's public key, created when a deployment is activated.
  • Usage snapshots — periodic counts used to generate invoices, with the timestamp they were reported.
  • Licence records — issuance, renewal, suspension and revocation events.
  • Correspondence — what you send to the addresses on the contact page.

Personal data is incidental rather than central here: these are machine and organization identifiers, not end-user profiles. Where an identifier can be linked to an individual, it is treated as personal data.

2.3 Roles

For the vendor-side licensing and billing records described above, {{LEGAL_ENTITY}} acts as controller. For anything processed inside your own deployment — your audit log, your entitlements, your artifacts — you are the controller and we are not a processor, because we never receive it.

2.4 Your rights

Subject to applicable law you may request access, correction, erasure, restriction, portability, or object to processing. Write to {{EMAIL_PRIVACY}}.

A limit we will not paper over

The audit log is a hash chain: each entry commits to the one before it, so deleting or editing an entry is detectable — which is the entire point of it as evidence. That property is in direct tension with a right to erasure over any identifier recorded in it.

Deleting a row would break the chain and destroy the integrity guarantee for every entry after it. The intended resolution is to store identifiers in a form that can be rendered permanently unreadable without altering the chain, so an erasure request can be honoured without falsifying the record. That change is not yet implemented, and the audit log lives in your infrastructure rather than ours, so today this is a limitation you inherit as its controller. We would rather tell you than let you discover it during an audit.

2.5 Retention

Usage snapshots and invoices are retained for as long as needed to bill and to satisfy statutory record-keeping, which in most jurisdictions is measured in years rather than months. Deployment and licence records are retained for the life of the relationship. Correspondence is retained for as long as it is useful to the matter it concerns.
