{{VENDOR_NAME}} is a distribution, licensing and execution-control platform for proprietary software and AI models that run on infrastructure their owner does not control. It is built around a claim it can keep, and it is explicit about the one it cannot.

The problem

Software that leaves your servers stops being yours in any enforceable sense. A customer's hardware, their administrators, their backups, their forks. Once a build lands you cannot say with evidence who has it, whether it should still run, or what it did last Tuesday.

The usual answer is obfuscation sold as secrecy. It buys time against a casual look and nothing at all against a determined one, and every vendor who sells it as more than that is borrowing credibility they will have to repay.

What it enforces

  • Attribution. Every customer's copy is watermarked at multiple points and signed. A leaked artifact traces back to the customer it was issued to — source, native binary, JavaScript, JVM bytecode or model weights alike.
  • Authorization. Content keys are released just-in-time and only after an entitlement check, so a copy on disk is not the same thing as a copy that runs.
  • Revocation. A kill switch at the stable handle — customer, deployment or licence. The next run is denied. It does not require reaching the machine.
  • Evidence. A hash-chained, tamper-evident audit log whose verdict latches: once tampering is recorded the log reports as broken permanently. Exportable for SIEM and for auditors.

Where the ceiling is

Client-side execution cannot deliver mathematical secrecy. If code runs on a machine somebody else owns, that person can, with enough effort, recover what it does. No amount of packing, obfuscation or licence checking changes that, and any product claiming otherwise is describing a hope rather than a mechanism.

Two tiers exist precisely because of it. split keeps the valuable computation on your own server and calls it at runtime, so a fully reverse-engineered client is still missing the part that matters. enclave runs that computation inside a hardware trusted execution environment, attested before any key is released — the only configuration that offers real secrecy against a host who owns the box.

Stated plainly

The enclave tier's hardware attestation is not yet enforced in the shipping product. Its quote backend is scaffolding, and the tier is not sold as delivering secrecy today.

We would rather lose a deal to that sentence than win one without it.

The four tiers

openSigned and watermarked. Tamper-evident provenance — no confidentiality.
localEncrypted, decrypted in memory, gated per run. Raises extraction cost; a determined host owner can still recover it.

enclaveThe jewel runs server-side in a hardware TEE, attested before any key release.

How it is deployed

The control plane runs inside your infrastructure. Your keys, your entitlement database, your audit log. We do not hold your signing root, we cannot decrypt your artifacts, and there is no configuration in which your protected bytes pass through us.

What is hosted is the small vendor-side plane that licenses your deployment and meters it for billing. The heartbeat it sends carries usage counts only — never keys, never entitlement secrets, never artifact contents. A payload with anything secret-shaped in it is rejected before any state is touched. Even a fully compromised licensing service could mint licences and read usage totals, and nothing further.

Licence enforcement is deliberately fail-soft. A lapsed licence blocks new packaging and new entitlements; it never bricks a running deployment. A vendor who can stop your production is a risk you should not accept, so we made it something we cannot do.

Reach

Python, Rust, Go, JavaScript, TypeScript, JVM, .NET, native PE/ELF/Mach-O binaries, and ONNX or safetensors model weights. Distribution goes through the package managers your teams already use — the registry holds a launcher, never your protected bytes.
