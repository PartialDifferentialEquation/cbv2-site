How pricing works

There is no public price list, because there is no configuration where the number is the same for two customers. What is fixed is the shape of the bill, and that is worth stating up front:

Basis
A contract sets the term, the currency and the per-unit rates. Invoices are generated from the usage your deployment already meters — no separate reporting, no honour system.
Metered quantity
Peak, not cumulative. A billing period is charged on the highest count reached during it, so churning entitlements up and down does not inflate the invoice.
Lifecycle
Draft → issued → paid, with the underlying usage attached to the invoice, so any line can be traced back to the metering that produced it.
Payment
Recorded out of band — wire, ACH or purchase order. Net terms are set by the contract.
No payment processor
We do not collect or store card details and no third-party processor sits inside the trust boundary. That keeps the platform out of PCI scope and consistent with the keys-in-your-custody posture — the same reason the licensing plane never sees your keys.

Ask for a quote with your rough shape — number of protected artifacts, number of customer deployments, which tiers — and you will get a rate card rather than a range.

Before you ask

  • Export control. {{VENDOR_NAME}} is encryption software and is treated as controlled under the US Export Administration Regulations. Some destinations, end-users and end-uses are restricted regardless of commercial terms. Tell us where it will be deployed early — it is cheaper to find out now than at signature.
  • Regulated environments. If you need a FIPS-validated cryptographic module, say so at the outset: it constrains the build and the platform it runs on.
  • What we cannot promise. If your requirement is that a customer who owns the machine cannot recover your logic, read what the ceiling is before the call. That requirement is met by the split and enclave tiers or not at all.
